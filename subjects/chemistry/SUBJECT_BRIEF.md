# Chemistry subject brief

This file records learner-specific assumptions and evidence choices for this
collection. Reusable chemistry authoring judgment belongs in `DOMAIN_GUIDE.md`;
the explained curriculum belongs in `ROADMAP.md`; `subject.toml` is executable
truth.

## Learner and destination

- **Intended learner:** not yet specified. The curriculum therefore supports a
  cold-start learner entering at the foundational layer and does not assume an
  age, institution, credential goal, or prior chemistry course.
- **Current mathematical/tool mastery:** unconfirmed. Every mathematical or
  computing capability that a deck may assume is named as an external
  prerequisite from the staged curriculum catalog; all other tool knowledge is
  treated as unseen.
- **Current domain mastery:** none confirmed. Deck-level cold-start work must
  assume no chemistry beyond the declared prerequisite closure.
- **Requested destination and use horizon:** `whole-field`—a durable,
  multi-level map from chemical literacy through representative contemporary
  research routes. This destination determines the present map, not the
  learner's identity or a permanent ceiling.
- **Graduate or research focus branches:** none specified. The roadmap includes
  representative routes in synthesis/catalysis, measurement/imaging,
  theory/simulation/data, functional materials/interfaces, macromolecular and
  soft matter, chemical biology/therapeutics, and environmental/sustainable
  chemistry.
- **Deck granularity:** `course`; every proposed deck is estimated at 6–14
  coherent chapters.
- **Durable capabilities:** translate among particulate, molecular, symbolic,
  graphical, macroscopic, and experimental representations; predict and explain
  structure, properties, equilibria, rates, and reactivity; design and evaluate
  syntheses and measurements; reason safely about hazards and waste; analyze
  chemical data reproducibly; and judge literature claims within their
  uncertainty and domain of validity.
- **Deliberate exclusions:** full chemical-engineering transport, reactor, and
  plant design; licensed clinical decisions; exhaustive catalogs of compounds
  or named reactions; and any claim that SRS can substitute for supervised
  laboratory work, extended calculation, research, or design practice.

## Decisions requiring learner confirmation

These are genuinely personal choices and have not been invented:

1. preferred entry point after diagnostic review and any already-mastered
   external mathematics, physics, biology, or computing decks;
2. which specialization branches should be built first;
3. desired jurisdiction for safety, waste, occupational, pharmaceutical, and
   environmental regulation beyond globally useful principles;
4. notation or language preferences, accessibility needs, device constraints,
   and tolerance for calculation-heavy versus experiment-heavy emphasis;
5. whether the practical horizon is general literacy, formal coursework,
   industry preparation, graduate study, or a particular research program.

## Conventions and boundaries

- **Terminology and notation:** use current IUPAC terminology, nomenclature,
  quantities, symbols, and SI conventions as the default. State accepted local
  or historical alternatives when their distinction matters. Never grade a
  convention-dependent answer without naming the convention.
- **Structure representations:** distinguish composition, connectivity,
  stereochemistry, conformation, resonance contributors, orbital/model
  pictures, standardized identifiers, and three-dimensional conformers. No one
  representation is treated as the molecule itself.
- **Simplifications that must be labeled:** ideal gases and solutions,
  concentration in place of activity, isolated equilibrium, elementary-step
  rate laws, rigid or localized bonding pictures, single dominant conformers,
  continuum solvent, ground-state or Born–Oppenheimer models, classical nuclei,
  perfect crystals, equilibrium phases, and single-cause structure–property
  stories.
- **Claims requiring current verification:** atomic weights and evaluated
  constants; hazard classifications, exposure limits, SDS information, and
  disposal rules; regulatory and pharmaceutical status; environmental fate and
  toxicity; instrument capabilities; database records; named-reaction scope;
  materials performance; and active research-frontier claims.
- **Jurisdiction:** no jurisdiction has been selected. OSHA and US EPA sources
  below are evidence examples, not universal legal advice. Decks must identify
  the applicable authority and date for regulated claims.
- **Accessibility/device constraints:** unconfirmed. Until specified, require
  phone-width legibility, meaningful alt text, redundant non-color cues, clear
  stereochemical and graph labeling, and no reliance on interactive 3-D views
  as the sole explanation.

## Evidence authorities

- **Curriculum and professional expectations:** ACS Committee on Professional
  Training, the QAA Chemistry Subject Benchmark Statement, and RSC degree
  accreditation criteria. They inform capabilities and progression; they do
  not verify individual chemical claims.
- **Terminology, nomenclature, quantities, and standards:** IUPAC Color Books,
  recommendations, Gold Book, InChI and other relevant digital standards;
  evaluated metrology and property data from NIST and other named standards
  bodies.
- **Safety and environmental practice:** the controlling local regulator,
  institutional chemical-hygiene plan, current SDS and primary hazard sources;
  ACS safety guidance, OSHA, US EPA, and National Academies guidance provide
  reference frameworks where applicable.
- **Chemical records:** primary literature and evaluated databases take
  priority. PubChem is an aggregator whose contributor, record date,
  provenance, and license must be inspected rather than treated as one
  homogeneous authority.
- **Open educational sources:** OpenStax may support introductory scope and
  examples under its stated license. Original explanations and figures remain
  the default; no source is transcribed merely because it is viewable.

## Authentic practice outside SRS

Competence requires supervised in-person laboratory work with escalating
independence; pre-lab planning and risk assessment; notebook and ELN practice;
instrument operation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and calibration; extended
derivations and mixed quantitative problem sets; synthesis and characterization
projects; computational notebooks and code review; literature searches,
seminars, posters, oral defenses, and technical writing; team design and
incident-response exercises; and open-ended research with appropriate ethical,
safety, waste, and data oversight. Cards may prepare, cue, and help diagnose
these performances but cannot certify them.
