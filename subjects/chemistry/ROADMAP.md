# Chemistry learning roadmap

## Learner and destination

- **Long-term capabilities:** explain and predict chemical structure, properties,
  energy, rates, equilibria, and reactivity across representations; work and
  reason safely; design syntheses and measurements; analyze data reproducibly;
  and assess chemical literature and research claims with explicit uncertainty.
- **Requested destination:** `whole-field`, from cold-start literacy through a
  shared undergraduate foundation, upper-division breadth, graduate preparation,
  and representative research routes.
- **Focus branches:** none specified; all research routes below are proposals,
  not assumptions about the learner's interests.
- **Deck granularity:** `course`, with 6–14 estimated chapters per coherent deck.
- **Knowledge policy:** unconfirmed chemistry, mathematics, computing, physics,
  biology, laboratory practice, and research conventions are unseen. A deck may
  assume only its direct prerequisite closure.

## Why these are coherent decks

- **The cold-start split is functional.** `chemistry-literacy-and-safe-practice`
  builds the language, scale, evidence, and hazard decisions needed before
  `quantitative-general-chemistry` uses algebra across a denser model system.
  Combining them would exceed course scale; making literacy optional would let
  unexplained particle and safety representations leak into the quantitative
  course.
- **ABIOP is a spine, not five forced containers.** Analytical, biochemistry,
  inorganic, organic, and physical chemistry remain visible, while physical
  chemistry splits into thermodynamic/kinetic and quantum/spectroscopic courses
  because their mathematical grammars and culminating performances differ.
- **Laboratory practice has its own progression.** The first laboratory deck
  establishes hazard analysis, records, manipulation, and uncertainty. The
  graduate research-methods deck handles literature, controls, integrity, and
  reproducibility. Neither claims to replace hands-on work.
- **Measurement is staged.** Introductory analytical design, integrated
  structure determination, graduate measurement/chemometrics, and frontier
  imaging/sensing demand different source registers and levels of inference.
- **Materials branches do not create false prerequisites.** Solid-state,
  polymer/supramolecular, surface/colloid, and electrochemical chemistry can be
  learned independently after their true shared foundations. Their graduate and
  research routes recombine only where multiscale practice requires it.
- **Green chemistry is not merged with environmental chemistry.** Product and
  route design uses synthesis and process metrics; environmental fate and
  exposure uses analytical, equilibrium, transport, and systems evidence. They
  converge only in research that genuinely spans both.
- **Application branches stay bounded.** Medicinal, nuclear, industrial, and
  chemical-biology decks each have a distinct practice portfolio. Full clinical
  practice and chemical-engineering design remain outside this subject.
- **All estimates pass the requested stress test.** Every proposal is 8–14
  chapters; no deck is a one-topic fragment or an “advanced topics” catch-all.

## Hard prerequisites, recommendations, and external reuse

- Generic arithmetic, algebra, calculus, differential equations, probability,
  statistics, numerical work, and programming are imported from canonical
  `mathematics/...` decks. Chemistry decks teach transfer into activities,
  partial-derivative thermodynamics, structures, spectra, kinetics, and chemical
  data rather than duplicating the general capability.
- Physics decks are normally `recommended_after`. Chemistry can teach the
  bounded physical models required for its own representations, so requiring a
  complete mechanics, electromagnetism, or physics-quantum route would create
  false prerequisites. The corresponding physics routes remain valuable for
  deeper interpretation.
- Biology decks are likewise recommendations for cell, molecular, protein, and
  ecosystem context. A local `biochemistry` deck is retained because ACS treats
  biochemistry as a chemistry foundation and chemistry-centered transfer
  requires mechanism, energetics, synthesis, and measurement representations.
  Requiring the broader biology prerequisite chain would be a false dependency.
- `chemical-data-computation-and-information` imports generic mathematical
  computing, then teaches chemical identifiers, provenance, structure search,
  and record semantics. The later computational decks therefore do not repeat a
  broad programming bridge.
- Direct hard edges are minimal: no deck lists an ancestor already supplied by
  another direct prerequisite. Helpful breadth, conventional course order, and
  cross-disciplinary context are recommendations.

## Cross-deck concepts

- **Scale and amount:** entities, mole, concentration, activity, interfaces,
  particle-size distributions, and system boundaries recur with increasing
  rigor.
- **Structure–property–reactivity:** composition, connectivity, stereochemistry,
  conformation, electronic structure, packing, defects, and morphology must be
  translated rather than collapsed into one picture.
- **Energy and time:** thermodynamic possibility, equilibrium position, rate,
  mechanism, transport, and observation time are repeatedly discriminated.
- **Evidence and inverse inference:** a spectrum, image, chromatogram, rate law,
  simulation, or biological response constrains hypotheses; it rarely identifies
  a unique molecular story alone.
- **Uncertainty and provenance:** calibration, traceability, sampling,
  significant figures, model error, metadata, versioning, and contributor
  provenance mature from literacy through research.
- **Safety, hazard, risk, and sustainability:** intrinsic hazard is not risk;
  exposure, scale, controls, waste, energy, life cycle, and alternative design
  must be revisited in every experimental and application branch.
- **Representation grammar:** formulae, equations, mechanisms, stereochemical
  drawings, orbitals, energy diagrams, spectra, diffraction patterns, phase
  diagrams, reaction networks, structures/identifiers, tables, and code each
  have explicit conventions and limits.

## Practice outside SRS

- supervised laboratory manipulation, instrumentation, calibration,
  maintenance, troubleshooting, waste handling, and emergency response;
- extended derivations, multi-hour mixed problems, synthesis planning, and
  open-ended design under realistic constraints;
- notebooks and ELNs, computational notebooks, version control, code review,
  data stewardship, and reproducibility exercises;
- literature searches, patent and structure searches, seminars, journal clubs,
  peer review, reports, proposals, posters, oral defenses, and audience-specific
  communication;
- team projects, incident analysis, life-cycle and hazard assessment, scale-up
  reviews, and decisions involving social, environmental, economic, ethical,
  and regulatory constraints;
- mentored research in which failed experiments, ambiguous measurements, and
  alternative hypotheses are handled rather than edited out.

## Deliberate extension points

Deferred application sectors can later branch from the analytical, organic,
biochemical, materials, and research-method layers without renaming approved
decks. Astrochemistry and extreme-conditions work should import the relevant
physics/astronomy capabilities when a catalog entry exists. Chemistry education,
history, policy, and public communication deserve coherent future decks rather
than fragments hidden in technical courses. No deferred label limits who may
study the material.
