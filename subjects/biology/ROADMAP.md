# Biology learning roadmap

## Learner and destination

- Long-term capabilities: explain living systems across scales; reason from
  evidence and uncertainty; translate among authentic biological
  representations; design and critique investigations; and enter selected
  advanced literature without hidden prerequisite gaps.
- Requested destination: `whole-field`, built for a cold-start learner and
  extending through representative graduate and research-facing routes.
- Focus branches: none specified. Advanced routes are deliberately balanced
  across molecular/cellular, organismal, ecological/evolutionary, and
  bioengineering directions.
- Deck granularity: `course`, with 6–14 estimated chapters per coherent deck.

The order values below are unique topological positions, not a command to study
all 35 decks linearly. Complete the shared core, then follow only the branches
needed for the learner's goals. Tier expresses priority for this whole-field
destination; level expresses assumed maturity.

## Layered route

1. **Cold-start bridge (orders 1–4).** Begin biology immediately while building
   quantitative, chemical, and statistical foundations in explicit parallel
   courses. Chemistry depends on quantitative foundations; introductory biology
   does not.
2. **Shared biological core (orders 5–13).** Cells, molecules, inheritance,
   evolution, ecology, biodiversity, research practice, and computation form a
   branching major-level base. The order allows molecular/cellular and
   ecological/evolutionary work to progress without forcing either through the
   other.
3. **Breadth branches (orders 14–22).** Microbial, plant, animal,
   developmental, neural, behavioral, immune, conservation, and genomic routes
   reuse only the portions of the shared core they genuinely need.
4. **Undergraduate specializations (orders 23–26).** Systems, structural,
   synthetic, and human-disease biology deepen distinct methods and culminating
   performances rather than forming one vague “advanced topics” deck.
5. **Graduate integrations (orders 27–31).** Representative pairings connect
   evolution with genomics or development, microbes with ecology, conservation
   with forecasting, and systems with engineering.
6. **Research-facing extensions (orders 32–35).** Single-cell/spatial biology,
   engineering living systems, global-change biosphere science, and origins of
   life show credible literature-facing endpoints. They are extension points,
   not permanent limits on the field.

## Coherence and false-prerequisite audit

Every proposal has one course-scale culminating capability and falls within the
6–14 chapter constraint. The following design decisions address the main
coherence risks across the complete set:

| Deck family checked | Stress-test result |
|---|---|
| Orders 1–4 | Biology starts without chemistry or mathematics. Quantitative foundations, chemistry, and statistics remain separate because they have different representations and capstones; statistics does not become a false prerequisite for elementary genetics or ecology. |
| Orders 5–8 | Cell biology, biochemistry, genetics, and molecular regulation are separate mechanism courses. Their hard edges reflect full-course dependencies, and no ancestor is repeated. |
| Orders 9–11 | Evolution, ecology, and biodiversity are not fused into “organismal biology.” Evolution is hard for phylogenetic interpretation; it is only recommended for ecology, which can establish its own minimal evolutionary bridge. |
| Orders 12–13 | Research practice and computation are explicit tool courses. Research practice owns design, safety, field/lab workflow, literature, and reproducibility; computation owns programming and data workflows. Neither is silently granted by learner level. |
| Orders 14–21 | Microbiology, plants, animal physiology, development, neuroscience, behavior, immunology, and conservation each use distinct source registers, representations, and culminating performances. Neuroscience is not made a prerequisite for behavior; microbiology is recommended rather than required for immunology. |
| Orders 22–26 | Genomics, systems modeling, structural biology, synthetic biology, and human disease are split because their data types and capstones differ. Programming and statistics are hard requirements only where independent quantitative analysis is central. |
| Orders 27–31 | Each graduate deck joins a specific pair or trio of established disciplines around an authentic integration; none is a generic “advanced biology” container. |
| Orders 32–35 | Each research extension is literature-facing and method-bounded. These decks demonstrate growth paths without claiming exhaustive frontier coverage. |

Direct hard edges are minimal: no listed prerequisite is already guaranteed by
another direct prerequisite. Helpful examples and conventional ordering use
`recommended_after` instead of granting blanket inbound knowledge.

## Cross-deck concepts

- **Evolution and variation:** first oriented in introductory biology, made
  mechanistic in genetics and evolution, then reused in biodiversity,
  microbiology, behavior, conservation, development, genomics, and origins.
- **Structure and function:** recurring translations span biomolecules,
  membranes, cells, tissues, organisms, neural circuits, communities, and
  engineered systems.
- **Information:** distinguish storage, transmission, expression, signaling,
  measurement, and inference rather than using one metaphor for all of them.
- **Energy and matter:** follow conservation, transformations, gradients,
  fluxes, and coupling from chemistry and metabolism through physiology,
  ecosystems, and biogeochemistry.
- **Systems and scale:** revisit boundaries, stocks and flows, feedback,
  emergence, heterogeneity, spatial structure, timescale, and model validity.
- **Evidence and uncertainty:** controls, sampling, measurement, causal limits,
  model comparison, reproducibility, and communication recur with increasing
  independence.
- **Science and society:** ethics, history, equity, sustainability, governance,
  dual use, and consequences appear where real biological decisions arise.

## Practice outside SRS

The curriculum requires laboratory and field work, extended quantitative and
computational analyses, open-ended modeling, primary-literature reading,
writing, presentations, collaboration, and an independent investigation.
Flashcards can support the concepts and decisions used in those performances
but cannot certify hands-on technique, research independence, clinical skill,
or professional compliance. The detailed practice contract and confirmation
items are in `SUBJECT_BRIEF.md`.
